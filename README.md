## Current Focus: The Help Desk Project

**[The Help Desk Project](https://github.com/ryan-sharpnack/the-help-desk-project)**

An open-source, community-driven initiative designed to bridge the gap between learning IT and actually doing IT.

The project focuses on creating realistic IT support scenarios, resolving them in homelab environments, and documenting the troubleshooting process so aspiring IT professionals can develop practical, employer-relevant skills.

The goal is to bring together:

- **Aspiring IT Professionals** — Build homelabs, work through realistic support tickets, develop troubleshooting skills, and demonstrate practical experience.
- **IT Professionals** — Contribute real-world scenarios, share technical knowledge, provide feedback, and help mentor the next generation.
- **Hiring Managers and IT Leaders** — Share common support challenges and help identify the practical competencies expected of new IT professionals.

The vision is to help develop IT professionals who can contribute confidently, solve problems independently, and bring practical experience to the workplace from Day One.

## Long-Term Career Direction: Principal OT Network Engineer

My long-term objective is to become a Principal OT Network Engineer, specializing in the design, implementation, security, and engineering of industrial network infrastructure.

I intend to develop toward this goal through a deliberate progression:

1. **IT Support** — Re-enter the IT industry, strengthen my operational experience, and develop a deeper understanding of enterprise IT environments.
2. **Enterprise Networking** — Advance into network engineering, building expertise in routing, switching, enterprise infrastructure, network security, and automation.
3. **Industrial Networking** — Transition into OT environments and develop expertise in industrial network architecture, protocols, infrastructure, and security.
4. **Principal OT Network Engineering** — Progress toward advanced technical leadership, complex infrastructure design, engineering strategy, and enterprise-scale industrial network solutions.

I believe strong industrial network engineers need more than theoretical networking knowledge. They need a solid foundation in IT, enterprise infrastructure, and the operational realities of the environments they support.
